03 The core idea

Having access to a screen is not the same as having the authority to decide.

Most systems stop at "this user has the approve permission". HeartStone keeps these concepts separate in its data model and its code. It never merges them for convenience.

User≠Person≠Officeholder≠Role≠Permission≠Authority
Built

Who are you?

Authentication. Sign-in through an OpenID Connect identity provider, with step-up verification for sensitive actions.

Built

What may your account open?

Technical access. Roles and permissions, deny-by-default, scoped to an institution, with access levels A–F.

The difference

May you make this decision?

Institutional authority. The engine checks the office, appointment, delegation, governing law and conditions at the moment of the decision, and fails closed.

Before any consequential action, the platform asks:

  • Which office is acting, and is the appointment valid today?
  • Which governing source grants this power?
  • Does the delegation cover this service, case type and amount?
  • Is the required evidence present and verified?
  • Is a second approval needed? Is it self-approval?
  • Is there a conflict or recusal?
  • Is a national or external determination required?
  • Is the service operationally activated, not suspended?

AI agents and service accounts are denied decide, sign, issue and approve actions outright.

05 How a case moves

From "I need a permit" to a verifiable licence.

Automated steps prepare the work, and only an authorised person makes the decision.

Find the service

The citizen describes what they need. HeartStone works out the service, the department and what's required.

Citizen

Apply once

A versioned form, saved as a draft. On submission it is fingerprinted and tied to the version of the service it was made under.

Citizen

Evidence checked

Documents are scanned and stored encrypted, then marked "not yet checked" until an officer verifies them. Uploading a document doesn't make it verified.

Officer

Reviews run

Sequential and parallel reviews, completeness checks, and referrals to other departments.

Departments

Packet frozen

The evidence the decision relies on is frozen into a packet with a SHA-256 manifest, so it can't change afterwards.

Platform

Authority checked

A fresh evaluation runs at the moment of decision: office, appointment, delegation, conditions, conflicts. Any doubt means no.

Authority engine

Human decision

An authorised official makes the decision, and it is recorded and hashed. Paying a fee is never the same as approval.

Officeholder

Issued & verifiable

The licence is signed through a trust service and can be checked publicly. Renewal, suspension and appeal follow from it.

Issuing office
Consequential steps are written to the audit ledger. Who acted, under which office and authority, on which evidence, under which workflow version, and what changed. The ledger is hash-chained, so any tampering shows.

07 Governed AI on S3M

AI that assists government without ever becoming the government.

HeartStone treats AI as a governed machine identity, not a chatbot bolted on. The control plane is built into the backend. S3M, Abraxas's sovereign AI engine, is designed to be the model it governs.

Who asks

Officials, analysts and registered AI agents.

  • Summarise a case
  • Check completeness
  • Find inconsistencies
  • Draft correspondence
  • Prepare a decision brief
  • Brief the executive command view
Built in the backend

HeartStone AI control plane

  • Each AI agent has its own identity, separate from people and service accounts
  • Registries of approved model providers, models, versions and agents
  • Policy gate checks every call and denies when in doubt
  • Data-class limits per agent, independent of the user's own access
  • Tool allowlists, which never create legal authority
  • Human review and edit history before anything reaches the public
  • Every call recorded in the audit ledger: model, version, inputs, sources, output, reviewer
  • Suspend any agent, model or provider instantly

S3M

Sovereign AI engine

  • Sovereign AI architecture from Abraxas
  • CPU-optimised: designed to run on standard in-country servers, not specialist GPU clusters
  • Developed for defence use, at technology readiness level 6
  • Open-source intelligence and common operational picture dashboards, a natural fit for the executive command centre
  • Connects through HeartStone's model port, like any approved provider
AI can never:APPROVEDECIDESIGNISSUEENFORCESUSPENDREVOKEHEAR A REVIEW
Where this stands. The control plane, registries, policy gate and audit are built and tested. External AI is switched off by default, and the backend runs a placeholder model until a provider is approved. Connecting S3M means writing its adapter for the model port, registering it, and having the government approve it.

Why this matters: the government chooses and controls its AI. Models can be swapped or suspended without changing the platform, and sensitive data never has to leave sovereign infrastructure.

08 What's new

Six ideas most e-government platforms don't have.

i.

Authority-aware, not just permission-aware

Legal authority is modelled from the governing law down to the officeholder, and checked at the moment of every consequential action.

ii.

Evidence as a first-class record

Provenance, verification state, custody and frozen packets mean any decision can be reconstructed and defended years later.

iii.

A new service is configuration, not a new project

Service packs describe forms, evidence, fees, workflow and authority, and compile into governed services. 22 templates exist today.

iv.

AI as a governed machine identity

Registered, scoped, audited, suspendable, and never a decision-maker. Built for sovereign models like S3M.

v.

The right to stop

Services must be activated with evidence and can be suspended. If authority, identity, evidence or security can't be established, processing halts safely.

vi.

Tamper-evident institutional memory

A hash-chained audit ledger, separate from technical logs, records who did what, under which authority.
